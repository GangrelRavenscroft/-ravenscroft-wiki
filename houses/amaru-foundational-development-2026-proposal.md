## Proposed defining identity

**Working description: The House of the Watersheds** — a modern editorial epithet, neither a historical title nor heraldic motto.

Amaru draws exceptional durability from linking the **Andean highlands, southern Peru, the Bolivian Altiplano, the Pacific export corridor, and adjacent river basins** through water, energy, highland industrial services, logistics, agriculture, legal expertise, finance and personal obligations. Networks of separately owned land, trusts, professional institutions, subordinate families and autonomous regional allies amplify its influence. No House owns Peru or Bolivia, all public institutions, a whole culture or all traditional sites.

Amaru is not simply 'secret Inca royalty' or a benign conservation society. Its surviving narrative includes difficult colonial accommodations, contested labor and resource relationships, community obligations, legal conflict and periods when family advantage and public welfare diverged. Document outcomes and consequences with nuance.

## Proposed institutional structure (no invented company ownership)

1. Water infrastructure, watershed monitoring and irrigation engineering, with lawful local consent and water rights.
2. Energy assets, mineral *processing*, highland industrial logistics and technical-services companies.
3. Inland trade corridors, port access through commercial contracts, insurance and contingency logistics.
4. Agriculture, conservation, land stewardship and cultural scholarships.
5. Patient family capital, diverse lawful trusts, legal-arbitration expertise and diplomatic patronage.

All are **proposed sectors**, not named House-owned conglomerates. Do not confuse net worth, gross enterprise assets, client AUM, state infrastructure or affiliated Houses' fortunes. **Principal Amaru wealth remains unassigned**.
