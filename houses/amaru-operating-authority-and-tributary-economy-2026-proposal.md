## Executive concept: a Great House as a layered jurisdiction, not a conglomerate

Amaru's plausible governing principle is **central authority over what could endanger the dynasty, with broad delegated control of matters that only regional actors understand**. A rural Lesser House, a port-connected Major House, an overseas investment officer, and a hidden criminal patron do not have identical jobs, charters, ownership or information access. They answer to the same sovereign apex only when their actual affiliation requires it.

This can make Amaru exceptional in its home terrain: **financially smaller than several European peers but more deeply informed about local networks**. Its global portfolio is economically valuable, while its regional authority involves reciprocal commitments, independent stakeholders, local knowledge, contractual influence, and (for genuinely bound networks) enforceable criminal tribute. The Great House must not be portrayed as literally owning Peru, Bolivia, their populations, protected Indigenous cultural property, public water, or national infrastructure.

## 1. What stays central and what stays local — recommended decision matrix

| Issue | Default first responsibility (proposal) | Senior-House reserved authority (proposal) |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary administration of a Major House's chartered region | The Major Head and appropriately delegated local officers | Review charter violations, competing mandates or unusual risks; do not automatically manage routine business |
| Local Lesser House obligations | That Lesser's immediate superior (Major or Great House, as chartered) | Final constitutional appeal for disputes about who has jurisdiction |
| Public-facing commercial contracts and engineering projects | The actually contracted company, its board, professional managers and local House contact if appropriate | Intervene only where a genuine House-common strategic decision or reserved obligation applies |
| Senior/common House trusts and reserved family patrimony | The legally empowered fiduciaries and senior common-House officers | Approve constitutional changes affecting common property where permitted; never treat all affiliated assets as personal property |
| Inter-House compact commitments | Authorized senior House representatives; Alejandro's existing external liaison role is relevant | Reserved approval for commitments that bind sovereign Amaru |
| Order-wide emergencies and Compact questions | Authorized House security/Order channels | Recognize the Order's distinct jurisdiction, Conclave and elected Nine; Amaru doesn't replace them |
| Bound criminal enterprise's ordinary operations | That enterprise's own hierarchy, accountable to its immediate superior | Great-House ultimate authority over allegiance, exceptional disobedience or dynastic risk |
| Tribute disputes or affiliated underworld rivalry | Immediate supervisory layer with local knowledge | Senior reserved adjudication for sustained refusal, strategic exposure, large inter-jurisdiction disputes or threats to House primacy |
| Matters involving independent criminal groups | No inherent House superior; relationships, if any, depend on the actual agreement | Cannot claim vassal authority solely from geography, rumor or receipt of an isolated payment |
| Succession continuity | Principal heir, authorized fiduciaries and established family procedures, details still to be settled | The formally constituted House Head and relevant constitutional/Order rules |

**Draft constitutional rule:** Every recognized Major House is answerable directly to Amaru, never another Major; a Lesser House is subordinate to its actual Great/Major superior. Both levels can administer portions of their own jurisdiction without distributing it entirely among subordinates. A Great House can also manage direct local assets and direct Lessers. None of these House relationships replaces national public law.

## 3. Tribute is authority and income, not an automatic dynasty fortune

The **locked world-wide rule** is upward payments/obligations within *bound* criminal affiliations: groups retain normal operational independence, remit obligations to their immediate superior, and the agreed eventual value reaches the Great House through intermediate layers. Superior Houses may demand compliance and impose serious consequences. The exact Amaru percentages, amount, mechanisms and affiliated groups are **not established**.

A sound Amaru wealth ledger should show **four different registers** even if a story later brings them into conflict:

1. **Beneficial net capital:** Principal/common House equity actually owned, net of attributed liabilities, within the **locked $350–650B** range; its proposed $500B midpoint is not yet an asset-by-asset canon ledger.
2. **Independently owned subordinate property:** Major/Lesser properties and operational firms do not become common-House holdings by constitutional supremacy alone.
3. **Broader assets influenced or controlled:** The **locked $1–2T** envelope, nonadditive and overlapping. It reflects contractual/strategic influence that may not belong to Amaru at all.
4. **Operating flows and underworld tribute:** Payments can move among organizations and Houses, with a share retained at each permitted level, but the same sum cannot become three different wealth assets merely because it crosses three layers. Without confirmed figures and ownership, **do not invent annual criminal receipts or roll every drug-market dollar into the principal net worth**.

Power from a criminal connection can greatly exceed its direct financial contribution. A highly influential but politically hazardous relationship might matter to Amaru even if it generates little net capital compared with its global equity holdings. This is an economic and political distinction, **not** a statement that a named cartel currently serves Amaru.

## 4. Coercion, obedience and the limits of central power

It is part of the **established world** that Great Houses have the personnel, finance, networks and coercive capacity to keep most subordinate enterprises in line. Affiliates who resist can face loss of protection, forced change in status, removal of leadership, or — at the ruthless end of the fictional political spectrum — assassination, fighting involving loyal criminal groups, or direct House intervention. The **existence of senior supremacy is locked**, but **the procedural thresholds and the personal decisions of Amaru's family are not**.

A distinctive proposed House-Amaru choice would be to regard escalating open conflict as strategically expensive: instability can threaten legitimate infrastructure investments, local House legitimacy, legal investigations and international finance. This might make its senior House severe in ultimate capacity yet restrained about using overt force. It would also create real disagreement between those seeking immediate disciplinary action and those prioritizing durable stability. **No specific reprisal, assassination, civilian harm, syndicate conflict or House Head order is being added to historical canon.**

In an appropriately layered narrative, a street-level group may not know its syndicate's true superior, the syndicate may know only a Lesser House contact, and even the Lesser House may lack knowledge of senior classified Order affairs. Criminal payment, affiliation, constitutional House membership and Order induction are **not interchangeable**.

## 5. Three potential internal crises (fictional future story prompts, not past events)

**A. The unpaid obligation.** A bound, unnamed underworld organization withholds payments during a local power dispute, claiming the local superior has breached its reciprocal duties. The Lesser or Major must resolve the matter within its competence, but the senior House is asked whether honoring the lower relationship would endanger the authority of the entire network. The interesting conflict is between legitimate contractual grievance and the need to preserve ultimate dynastic supremacy; do not invent criminal enforcement steps or an approved assassination.

**B. The conflicting mandate.** A Major House's private engineering investment and another Major's commercial interest are affected by the same contested public resource or route. Their internal House responsibilities overlap but neither Major outranks the other. A senior constitutional decision may be required, while public agencies and independent communities retain agency outside the fiction's hidden hierarchy. This would test Nicolás's practical competence without fabricating a new official post for him.

**C. The dangerous ally.** An independently organized criminal-business enterprise offers lucrative cooperation to an Amaru-affiliated institution but refuses any binding superior authority. Some family members prefer the financial opportunity, others see unacceptable exposure. The crucial canon question becomes *whether there is any affiliation at all*, not how to control a criminal group the House does not presently command.

These are **not events canonically dated or attributed to any family member**. They can later become dossier stories, official House incidents or image subjects only after review.
