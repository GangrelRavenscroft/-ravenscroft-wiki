## I. Governing distinction: geographic contact is not constitutional command

De Villiers comprises sovereign senior House, two French blood-cadet Major Houses, four non-blood bound Major Houses, and directly or regionally subordinated Lesser Houses. Each Major reports DIRECTLY to Great House de Villiers; a Major may govern Lessers but never another Major. Each House has its own lawful property and internal constitution. Within the Belgian/Swiss networks the already **locked** local Major Heads are Geneviève Thérèse de Bréval and Étienne Marc de Corvelles.

1. **Constitutional authority:** loyalty, protected-house records, safety/continuity assistance, dispute submission and obligations of recognized House members.
2. **Corporate control:** only actual shares, voting rights, trust mandates, contracts and company boards — not the mere fact that a House services a region.
3. **Professional project command:** lead engineer, contractor, board and public owner authority on a specific project, even where those professionals belong to different Houses.
4. **Political and institutional access:** negotiated relationships with independent governments, industry, universities and financial institutions; do not describe these as House territory.
5. **Security authority:** internal personnel/property protection and reciprocal crisis arrangements; not blanket law-enforcement powers, industrial espionage privileges or unlimited classified access.

**Suggested charter maxim:** *Authority follows the charter; responsibility follows the work.* Not yet an approved House motto.

## II. Proposed consultation and dispute structure

The **existing seven-seat Conseil de Maison is locked**: Philippe as Head, Adrien as designated successor, Henri as senior strategic adviser, two blood-cadet Major seats, an unfilled professional Steward of Capital and an unfilled constitutional Warden. Charles is nonvoting elder. No automatic enlargement to accommodate four bound Major Heads.

### Create a subordinate *Conférence des Maisons Associées* — NOT YET CANON

A practical advisory assembly for the six Major Houses (two blood cadets and four bound Majors). Its remit is consultation, service burden allocation, crisis lessons, compliance standards and reciprocal works agreements. It is **not another sovereign House Council** and may not override the Conseil de Maison.

- **Membership proposal:** each of the six Major Heads may attend personally or delegate one accredited representative; House de Villiers supplies a professional secretary and a presiding Senior House representative appointed by Philippe.
- **Meetings:** quarterly plus extraordinary protected-risk consultation. Location alternates among Paris, Brussels, Geneva and regional seats where practicable.
- **Decision mechanism:** consensus advice where possible; majority recommendations may be sent to Conseil but do not become a charter change automatically.
- **Transparency:** if a proposed common project creates risks for a subordinate, the senior House must disclose the nature of guarantees, project liabilities, environmental exposure and governing contracts before requesting approval.
- **Due process:** an affected Major cannot be compelled simply by a Council vote to transfer its separately owned beneficial assets, except as already specified by a lawful, consented constitutional instrument.

### Draft incident/escalation sequence — NOT YET CANON

**Ordinary issue:** direct affected-company or contractual team negotiation → regional Major advisers → optional independent engineering/financial determination → House reciprocal committee → Philippine sovereign office / Conseil of the legally competent category. For an actual Compact or inter-Great-House dispute, a separate Order Tribunal can have jurisdiction under existing world law.

**Emergency:** protect life and property immediately through lawful local responders and the actual site owner/contractor; notify both affected Major offices and the senior House protected continuity officer. Urgent field measures cannot be used to permanently rewrite ownership or constitutional superiority. Independent professional safety findings remain reportable.

**Conflict-of-interest:** professionals sign actual engineering decisions based on their credentials and evidence; an invested dynast cannot certify away adverse technical findings. House de Bréval or de Corvelles can refuse credit guarantees not validly authorized by their own constitutions.
