## 1. Constitutional identity and distinction

**LOCKED:** House de Villiers is an autonomous, recognized **Great House**, one of the eleven peers of the hidden international Order in 2026. It is not subordinate to another French House. The discarded authoring name *de Varenne* has **no in-world House, genealogy, heraldry, head or historic replacement narrative**.

**LOCKED House identity:** *The Architects of Continuity* is the House's defining epithet and organizing principle, not yet an approved French heraldic motto. It specializes in **physical systems and their continuity**: civil engineering, durable infrastructure, energy and water, rail/roads, maintenance, built heritage, urban resilience, reconstruction, and capital planning over generations. The theme does not confer ownership over France, its state agencies or particular public infrastructure.

**LOCKED cultural character:** cultivated, technically rigorous, diplomatically fluent, design-conscious, patient in long-term investment, preservation-minded, often slow to change. Core internal debate: stewardship of inherited systems versus needed modernization; professionals must retain genuine competence and institutional independence.

**LOCKED foundational geography / working sites:**
- **Château de Montrevaux**: fictional constitutional and ancestral seat in Burgundy, France, in the Côte-d'Or sphere; exact municipality and architectural/history detail still to design.
- **Hôtel de Villiers, Paris**: administrative / institutional center; a distinct working headquarters, not a second sovereign seat.
- Core Burgundian / Parisian professional and landed relationships. Broader French and European connections are institutional, commercial, patronage or allied influences **not claimed public sovereignty**.
- Henri/Catherine household properties in Manhattan, Hudson Valley, Paris and *Moulin des Ormes* (Burgundy) remain distinct residences; none is by implication common sovereign House property.

**LOCKED House historic dignity as a social/dynastic style:** *Marquis de Montrevaux*. In republican France, treat this as historical/house and social usage, **not a claim to modern governmental noble authority**. Charles is the living holder; Philippe is constitutional House Head but does not automatically hold his father's inherited dignity while Charles lives. Its exact creation instrument and earlier titled holders are still open.

**Not yet approved:** constitutional quorum, majority/supermajority thresholds, acting council composition before all seats are filled, emergency powers, charter revisions, conflict-of-interest procedure, regency and removal of Head. Do **not** import Valdieri's five-of-seven + Head rule.

**International Council of Nine:** mandate status, Councilor and Deputy Councilor **not yet selected**. Great-House status does not grant an automatic Nine mandate.
**Order standing:** Henri's Tier II status is an individual standing, not a Nine seat or constitutional Headship.
