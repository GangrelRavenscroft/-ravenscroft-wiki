## 1. Constitutional model and regional allocation

2026 design objective: make the House materially powerful without fictional ownership of all French utilities or territory. Its influence is a layered constitutional and commercial structure.

**Locked rank law applied to de Villiers:**
- The senior **Great House** stands independent, led by Philippe Étienne de Villiers. His House must maintain the expertise and security capacity to act without perpetual sponsorship from Ravenscroft.
- All **Major-ranking cadet Houses and non-blood bound Major Houses answer directly to Great House de Villiers**. One Major cannot constitutionally control another Major.
- **Cadet** means separate chartered blood dynasty (of Major or Lesser rank), **not** an extra rank. A branch of blood relatives is not necessarily its own House.
- **Lesser Houses** serve a town / corridor / cluster of local relations or a specialist field. The direct superior must be identified. Their professional projects can travel beyond a superior's region without altering allegiance.
- A regional sphere denotes **jurisdiction over the de Villiers network**, not public governmental territory or ownership of all businesses in the province.
- Other Great Houses, independent French firms and public authorities retain separate rights and relationships. Distinguish proprietary assets, chartered obligations, alliances and influence.

## 2. First cadet — de Villiers de la Loire (PROPOSED)

### Dynastic identity
A professionally pragmatic de Villiers blood line anchored in river corridors and the conversion of historic patrimonial transport networks to contemporary water-management, flood resilience, agricultural systems and river ecology. Prestige arises from reliable operation and expertly defended autonomous rights, not from imitation of the senior Château.

- **Head:** Victoire Madeleine de Villiers de la Loire (b. 12 May 1966; 60 in 2026). Civil engineer and hydrologic-risk negotiator, technically trained, notably good at translating flood-risk facts into acceptable finance and public-private maintenance contracts.
- **Spouse:** **Étienne Marc Lavigne**, b. **1965**; architect focusing on restoration and sustainable adaptive reuse; married-in, professional and independent, no assumed blood-right.
- **Children:** **Clémence Victoire** (b. **1994**; freshwater-systems scientist), **Mathis Étienne** (b. **1997**; municipal finance / river infrastructure analyst); heir designation OPEN.
- **Private seat:** **Domaine des Aulnes**, an expressly **fictional** Loire estate and working regional office, exact town pending.
- **Independent assets:** patient infrastructure-equity investment firms, long-leased commercial properties, proprietary engineering consulting and land trusts, not ownership of the River Loire or public dams.
- **Political interest:** insists that the two cadet council seats must be genuinely deliberative; guards branch capital against the senior line's grand projects.
- **Story vulnerability:** water scarcity and flood control create conflicting customers and public-interest obligations. Maintains close professional ties to environmental scientists even when this annoys old financiers.

## 7. Representation and constitutional tension

**Existing seven-seat Council is LOCKED; appointment identities to seats 4/5 are still PROPOSED.** If both proposed blood cadets are ratified:
- Seat 4 could be occupied by **Victoire Madeleine** as Head of de Villiers de la Loire.
- Seat 5 could be occupied by **Antoine Luc** as Head of de Villiers de Provence.
- Senior-line seats remain Philippe, Adrien and Henri; two professional officer seats remain unassigned.
- **d'Aubrecourt / Montferrand do not acquire those cadet seats**, but must receive charters providing consultation and appeals so nonblood Majors are not constitutionally invisible.
- The exact ordinary-vote and protected-decision thresholds are **not approved**. Do not silently assume Valdieri's 5/7 + Head concurrence rule.
- Cadet blood, marriage, Order membership, formal office, wealth, institutional access and security clearance must remain separate dimensions.

### Draft cadet charter limits
A Major-ranking cadet Head may:
1. Set ordinary budgets, choose personal advisers and manage **beneficially owned** branch assets.
2. Appoint internal branch staff and supervise chartered Lesser Houses.
3. Maintain ordinary commercial and professional relationships, including external Great House professional contracts within protected-decision rules.
4. Conduct local continuity operations, patronage and protective security within approved access.
5. Raise inter-House and House-constitutional concerns directly to Philippe's senior office, and appeal defined adverse acts under agreed procedural protections.

Reserved to Great House:
- Authenticating whole-House succession, defining common constitutional policy, acknowledging or amending branch charters under proper process, protecting shared House archives and fulfilling Order Compact obligations.
- Authorizing whole-House diplomatic undertakings. This is not unilateral ownership of individual cadet holdings, nor authority to command French state institutions.
- Cross-regional dispute resolution and strictly limited protected security coordination.

### Proposed unwritten loyalty system
De Villiers prefers **professional reciprocity** over pure ideological obedience: joint technical training, exchange posts, financing advantages, shared risk pools with separately documented ownership, marriage and cultural kinship, public recognition of cadet expertise, transparent internal arbitration and serious sanctions only after charter process. Vulnerability: lucrative competence creates genuine branch leverage; senior Head cannot assume every cadet will back a flawed project.

## 8. One proposed historical grievance — not yet an approved event

**Working event: the Loire Allocation Dispute (circa 1987–1991).** A predecessor of the Loire cadet disputed an attempt to pledge independently owned branch assets as security for a senior-backed transport and civil-works portfolio. No civil war or public scandal was necessary. A later House compact clarified branch beneficial ownership, required independent assessments before cross-guarantees and widened technical input to decisions. This could explain de Villiers's 2026 emphasis on documented obligation, rather than charity or coercion. **This event, dates, charter and predecessor identities are UNAPPROVED** and may need chronology adjustments against Charles's 1988 headship.

## 10. Finance discipline and size question
