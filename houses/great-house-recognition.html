Constitutional Principle

A Great House is recognized, not created.

Great-House standing is the Order's formal recognition that a dynasty has already become a durable sovereign peer within the hidden world. Wealth, antiquity, fame, or military strength alone are insufficient.

01

Peer Qualification Standard

All categories are material

Independent Sovereignty

The House must govern itself in fact, not merely in title. It must possess a stable Headship or equivalent constitutional authority and cannot function as a dependent cadet, client, or administrative arm of another House.

Durable Succession

Independence must survive leadership change. The Order looks for demonstrated continuity across multiple successions or an equivalent period of stable self-government. No fixed age alone guarantees or bars recognition.

Institutional Depth

The House must possess durable institutions beyond one charismatic leader or fortune: trusts, estates, companies, schools, foundations, archives, patronage systems, professional networks, or comparable structures capable of surviving generations.

Strategic Consequence

The House must matter beyond its immediate household. This normally means meaningful international reach, though exceptional regional consequence can satisfy the standard where the House is indispensable within a major sphere.

Independent Resources

The House must maintain sufficient diversified resources to remain autonomous through crisis. Raw net worth is not decisive; excessive dependence on a patron House, single state, single company, or single individual weighs strongly against recognition.

Protection & Security

The House must be able to protect its people, secrets, archives, assets, and obligations without routinely requiring another Great House to do so. This includes intelligence, counterintelligence, security, emergency continuity, and crisis response.

Network & Patronage

A Great House must command enduring relationships: members, allied families, protégés, patrons, institutional partners, and reciprocal obligations extending beyond blood relatives.

Order Reliability

The House must demonstrate sustained adherence to the Veil, the Compact, Tribunal judgments, and the Common Peace, together with the capacity to contribute treasury, personnel, sanctuary, intelligence, logistics, or other burdens to the shared Order.

Not a scorecard. Peer Qualification is holistic, but a dynasty cannot compensate for the absence of genuine independence or durable succession merely by being extraordinarily wealthy or dangerous.
02

Recognition Process

Petition

A Major House may seek Candidate Great-House status, normally with sponsorship from established peers or after the Order determines that its position requires formal review.

Examination

The Order examines governance, succession, finances, institutional resilience, international consequence, security, Compact compliance, and the House's ability to carry Great-House obligations.

Tribunal Finding

The Tribunal may find a House Unqualified, Candidate Qualified, or Peer Qualified. Peer Qualification is a legal finding that the objective standard has been met.

Conclave Recognition

The Conclave determines formal recognition. Existing Great Houses may resist or delay elevation, but the constitutional process is designed to prevent permanent exclusion of a dynasty that repeatedly demonstrates true peer status.

Accession

A newly recognized Great House enters the Compact as a constitutional peer, assumes the full burdens of Great-House standing, and receives one Voice in the Conclave.

Council Maturation

A newly elevated House normally serves twenty-one years in good standing before becoming eligible to contest one of the Nine Council mandates.

03

Historical Precedent — Ravenscroft

The lineage that became House Ravenscroft began as an American cadet branch of House Falkenrath. During the American War of Independence it broke from Falkenrath authority and established an independent House.

Independence did not immediately make Ravenscroft Great. The House spent the following decades proving that its sovereignty, institutions, succession, patronage, security capacity, and North American influence were durable. Its eventual early-nineteenth-century recognition became a major precedent for the principle that a parent Great House cannot permanently deny recognition to a cadet line that has demonstrably become an independent peer.

04

Loss of Standing

Council mandate

A Great House may lose a seat among the Nine without losing Great-House status. Council service is an elected mandate, not hereditary constitutional superiority.

Great-House degradation

Degradation is exceptional. It requires structural collapse of independent House existence or grave, persistent violations of the Compact. Temporary weakness, debt, political defeat, or an unpopular generation are not sufficient by themselves.

05

2026 Peer-Roster Stress Test

Historical dossiers still to be written

The eleven-house 2026 roster is treated as current canon. The purpose of the stress test is not to reopen recognition casually; it is to require each eventual House history to demonstrate the same constitutional standard. Where history is still undeveloped, the archive records the evidence that must later be supplied.

Ravenscroft

Standard demonstrated

Durable independence from Falkenrath, repeated succession, deep institutions, continental consequence, independent security and patronage, Great-House accession precedent, and postwar international primacy.

Wycliffe

Established peer • historical depth to document

Must preserve its already established British institutional core, long continuity, sovereign House governance, independent resources, international network, and sustained Order obligations.

de Villiers

Established peer • succession history to reconcile

Great-House status now belongs to de Villiers rather than de Varenne. Its future history must explain that constitutional identity cleanly, including the older de Varenne relationship without reducing de Villiers to a dependent cadet House.

Falkenrath

Established peer • historical depth to document

Its antiquity, old-blood prestige, former Ravenscroft cadet relationship, independent institutions, strategic resilience, and long participation in Order affairs make it a clear peer; exact Compact-era milestones remain to be written.

Volkov

Established peer • resilience case to document

Its eventual dossier must demonstrate that survival through regime change, war, territorial disruption, and political transformation produced durable sovereignty rather than mere endurance under stronger patrons.

Veyrholm

Established peer • institutional case to document

Its Gotland-centered identity must be supported by sufficient independent institutions, knowledge networks, security, resources, and international consequence to explain how a geographically compact House maintains peer standing.

Valdieri

Established peer • resource-independence case to document

Its history must show that financial and institutional reach is diversified, multigenerational, and backed by genuine House sovereignty rather than wealth alone.

Amaru

Established peer • regional-to-global case to document

Its dossier must demonstrate durable Andean/South American sovereignty, intergenerational institutional strength, independent security and patronage, and sufficient international consequence to justify peer standing beyond a merely regional power base.

Ashcroft

Established peer • highest documentation priority

Ashcroft currently has the thinnest developed historical foundation. Its future record must establish origin, sovereign base, succession continuity, institutions, independent resources, security capacity, strategic consequence, and Order service without relying on status by assertion.

Shen

Established peer • rise and predecessor history to document

Its history must explain how Shen became the recognized Chinese Great House, its relationship to older houses such as Han, the durability of its institutions through modern Chinese political upheaval, and the basis of its contemporary transnational influence.

Kuroda

Established peer • concentrated-power case to document

Kuroda must demonstrate why geographic concentration in Japan does not equate to limited consequence: deep institutional continuity, autonomous security, strategic maritime and international reach, patronage, and sustained Order contribution should support its peer standing.

Stress-test rule: no future House history may justify Great-House status with “ancient,” “wealthy,” or “powerful” alone. Every peer must have a credible answer for sovereignty, succession, institutions, resources, security, network, strategic consequence, and Compact reliability.
