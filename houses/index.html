Dynastic Directory

Constitutional peers and the Houses around them.

A Great House is not simply a rich or ancient family. It is a dynasty formally recognized by the Order as a durable sovereign peer capable of maintaining its own institutions, security, succession, international obligations, and place within the Compact.

How a House becomes Great

Peer Qualification examines sovereignty, succession, institutional depth, strategic consequence, independent resources, security, patronage, and Order reliability.

Council status is separate

Great-House recognition does not guarantee one of the Nine. Council mandates are elected responsibilities held by only nine Great Houses at a time.

Other Dynastic Statuses

Major Houses, Candidate Great Houses, Peer-Qualified candidates, Lesser Houses, cadet Houses, Former/Fallen Great Houses, and independent lineages all exist beneath or alongside the Great-House peerage. Great-House caliber power does not automatically produce constitutional recognition.

Current regional powers outside the eleven-house roster will be classified as their histories are developed rather than automatically promoted to Great-House status.
