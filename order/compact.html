Constitutional Foundation

The Compact governs the Houses. The Houses govern themselves.

The modern Compact is a consolidated body of centuries of agreements, revisions, judgments, accession articles, and protocols. It protects House sovereignty while limiting conduct that threatens the Common Peace, the Veil, or the survival of the wider Order. Within Order doctrine, surface governments and institutions do not supersede the Compact or lawful House authority.

The Veil

The hidden world is a common responsibility. No House may deliberately expose another House or the Order for advantage, coercion, revenge, or gain.

House Sovereignty

Recognized Great Houses govern lawful internal succession, family governance, common property, education, patronage, archives, appointments, and discipline subject to the Compact.

Equality of Standing

A House may be greater in power without being greater in right. Antiquity, wealth, influence, Council service, and military capability do not increase a Great House's single constitutional Voice in the Conclave.

The Common Peace

The Compact preserves rivalry but forbids ruinous war. Competition, intelligence activity, patronage contests, political maneuvering, alliance-building, and proxy pressure remain possible beneath the threshold of prohibited inter-House warfare.

Mutual Obligation

Great privilege carries common burden. Great Houses contribute resources according to capacity, including intelligence, sanctuary, logistics, treasury, personnel, medical support, archives, security, or diplomatic service.

Limits of Authority

Blood, House office, Order rank, clearance, wealth, patronage, and operational command are separate categories. Possessing information or influence does not itself create lawful authority.

01

House Loyalty & Compact Obligation

Primary loyalty belongs to the House. A Great House exists to preserve its bloodline, continuity, power, institutions, and freedom of action across generations. The Order is not a sovereign authority morally superior to the Houses; it is the common system the Houses maintain because none of them benefits from endless mutual destruction, uncontrolled exposure, or a world in which every dispute becomes existential.

The Compact therefore functions as a binding constitutional contract among sovereign Houses. A House may choose to violate it in defense of what it considers an overriding House interest, but that choice does not erase the breach: it exposes the House to sanctions, retaliation, loss of standing, or even degradation by its peers. The Compact is obeyed because long-term House survival normally depends on preserving the system.

Surface allegiances remain subordinate to House loyalty. The modern formulation is: “House before all; the Compact is how the Houses survive one another.”

02

Order Induction

Every person formally inducted into the Order receives instruction in the Compact and the Histories, regardless of House, rank, wealth, specialty, or eventual clearance. House education may prepare a candidate; formal induction is what binds the member to the shared constitutional framework.

Common Histories are taught to every inductee. Restricted Histories provide deeper political and operational context at higher levels of induction and clearance. Sealed Histories remain compartmented even among senior members.

Initial instruction tests judgment as well as memory: sanctuary, competing House and Order commands, Veil obligations, jurisdiction, succession crises, and the point at which private rivalry becomes an Order matter.

04

War, Proxy Conflict & General Cessation

The Compact does not treat surface wars as inherently unlawful or as matters requiring Order intervention merely because they are destructive, ideological, authoritarian, expansionist, or morally abhorrent by public standards. Great Houses have influenced the beginning, duration, direction, and settlement of wars throughout history, sometimes cooperatively and sometimes through opposing proxies. Rival Houses may seek advantage from the same conflict so long as their conduct remains within the Veil, the Common Peace, and other Compact limits.

The controlling question is whether the conflict remains tolerable to the Order as a whole. When the International Council determines that continued war has become materially contrary to shared Order interests, it may issue a General Cessation Directive by no fewer than seven of the nine Council votes. From that point, every House is bound not to prolong the conflict for private advantage and must employ its available influence toward bringing the war to an end. Houses retain discretion over the means, timing, negotiations, and protection of their own interests, but refusal to support the collective decision constitutes a Compact breach. The full Conclave may subsequently rescind, narrow, or materially alter a General Cessation Directive by eight of the eleven Great-House Voices; pending such action, the Council directive remains binding.

A House is not sanctioned because it backed the losing side, served an authoritarian regime, profited from wartime conditions, or opposed another House through proxies. The constitutional offense arises when loyalty to a surface belligerent, ideology, ruler, or state is placed above the Order—or when a House refuses a lawful Order-wide decision that the conflict must end.

05

Historical Development

The Compact predates House Ravenscroft. Its earliest forms arose from periods of destructive inter-House conflict in which vendetta, succession warfare, assassination, retaliatory exposure, and proxy violence threatened the survival of the hidden world itself.

The modern text is not a single medieval document frozen in time. Successive eras added rules for sovereignty, succession, sanctuary, Great-House recognition, Council governance, modern intelligence, global warfare, decolonization, digital security, and other conditions earlier generations could not have anticipated.

Exact early signatories and the final chronology of the oldest revisions remain under historical development in the archive.
