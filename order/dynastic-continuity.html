Ratified Postwar Constitutional Standard

A House may rule its branches; it must not fall to its own uncertainties.

The House Valdieri catastrophe taught the Order that neither antiquity nor wealth can compensate for irreconcilable succession claims and rival cadet command. These Articles establish essential safeguards while maintaining the sovereignty of every Great House.

01

Historical Background

The Articles of Dynastic Continuity emerged from the Valdieri civil war of 1934–1946, the wider European wartime crisis, and the contemporaneous review of House Falkenrath. The internationally recognized Great House Valdieri was torn apart after the Monferrato cadet line challenged lawful succession, was joined by Genova and fought against the senior line and ultimately Canavese. The principal House won the war at such cost that it was subsequently degraded to Major House. The fate of the extinct Monferrato bloodline and the lasting loss of Valdieri's Council mandate became warnings to every House.

The Conclave ratified a series of interlocking continuity standards between 1953 and 1956. This is the established period of adoption; the precise day and text of every adopting vote can be developed later. The Articles complement, but are not identical to, House Falkenrath's specific 1957 Restoration Articles.

02

Authenticated Succession

Recognized Great Houses must maintain ascertainable, independently verifiable rules for identifying a legitimate Head, direct successors and emergency regents. A succession emergency should not allow competing cadet lines to produce mutually exclusive sets of authentic-looking commands. Records are held through protected continuity arrangements rather than depending wholly upon one person's possession of a House archive.

03

Cadet Charters and Delegated Powers

Cadet lines remain genuine members of their House, with whatever resources, offices and protected relationships the senior House's lawful constitution assigns to them. Every Great House must be able to distinguish branch-owned property, stewardship obligations, security warrants, constitutional succession rights and authority expressly delegated by the Head. A branch cannot turn a commercial or security mandate into an automatic claim to independent governance.

04

Cadet Independence and Recognition

The Articles do not bar a cadet branch from becoming truly independent or later earning Great-House standing. House Ravenscroft's historical separation from Falkenrath remains a precedent. A former cadet must demonstrate lasting sovereignty, succession, institutions, resources and Compact compliance, and seek formal peer qualification; rebellion or control of valuable assets alone never automatically confers a Great-House Voice or Council mandate. Nor may a senior House indefinitely veto a genuinely eligible former cadet simply from dynastic pride.

05

Emergency Control and Institutional Continuity

Each peer must be able to identify who may authorize security orders, protect essential archives and perform Compact obligations during a contested succession. Internal disagreements must not multiply command chains indefinitely or place protected institutions under rival claims without review. Houses retain freedom to use councils, regencies, trustees, and other constitutional forms appropriate to their own histories.

06

House Sovereignty and Compact Review

The Articles establish minimum documentation, continuity and dispute-management expectations; they do not give the Council routine authority to choose a House Head, rewrite a family constitution or seize an autonomous cadet's property. Where factional violence threatens the Common Peace, the Conclave, the Council within its jurisdiction, and any competent constitutional tribunal may use lawful Compact procedures. The Conclave's peer-recognition and degradation powers remain distinct from Council mandates and their separate elections.

07

Different House Implementations

Ravenscroft: distributed continuity, intelligence compartmentalization and verified dynastic authority. Falkenrath: a reinforced chain of command and explicit cadet charters, developed alongside its independent 1957 Restoration Articles. Wycliffe: rigorous legal and fiduciary documentary mechanisms. Veyrholm: continuity controls over routes, infrastructure and communications. Valdieri: central authenticated succession records, clearer branch-resource distinctions, explicit separation of House Headship from delegated stewardship and restricted continuity controls.

Other Houses implement the same common principles through locally appropriate charters. This page establishes shared history and broad institutional direction without inventing the detailed words of every Great House's internal instruments.

08
