Constitutional Division

The Conclave defines the peerage. The Council governs the common affairs of the Order.

The two bodies overlap politically but serve different constitutional functions. Every Great House has one Voice in the Conclave; only nine Great Houses at a time hold Council mandates.

01

The Conclave of Great Houses

Function

The Conclave is the constitutional assembly of the recognized Great Houses. It decides matters that alter the rules or peerage itself: Great-House recognition and degradation, election of Council Houses, Compact amendments, confirmation of a Grandmaster, and major constitutional disputes.

Voting

Each recognized Great House has exactly one constitutional Voice. Antiquity, wealth, military strength, global reach, or Council service do not create extra votes.

Who exercises the Voice?

The House Voice belongs by right to the Head of the Great House. The Head may exercise it personally or formally accredit a Conclave Delegate to speak and vote for the House. An Alternate Delegate may also be accredited for continuity.

The vote belongs to the House, not the delegate. A Head may replace the delegate according to that House's own law. If the Head appears personally, the Head normally resumes exercise of the Voice unless authority has been expressly left with the accredited delegate.

Delegate eligibilityAdult recognized member of the House in full standing.
Order standingFully inducted, deeper induction complete, and trained in the Compact and Restricted Histories.
ConflictsNo person may exercise more than one Great House's constitutional Voice at the same time.
Blood requirementBlood descent is customary in many Houses but is not required by the Compact; married-in or otherwise formally integrated House members may qualify.
02

The International Council — The Nine

Function

The Council is the standing strategic and executive governing body for common Order affairs: international security, intelligence coordination, sanctions, shared operations, protected institutions, inter-House crises, emergency action, disputes that have crossed into Order jurisdiction, and collective determinations that a major surface conflict has become intolerable to the Order as a whole.

Mandates

A Council seat is a mandate held by a Great House, not personal property of a Councilor. The working constitutional model uses twenty-one-year House mandates, with three seats normally reviewed every seven years.

Who represents a Council House?

Each Council House appoints a Councilor and a Deputy Councilor. The Great House chooses them according to its own internal law. The Compact does not prescribe whether a Head, family council, senior branches, or another House institution makes the appointment.

Councilors serve seven-year appointments and may be renewed while their House continues to hold the mandate. The House may replace its Councilor without losing the Council seat. Individual discipline likewise does not automatically punish the House.

Councilor eligibilityFormal member of the Great House in full standing, fully inducted, with substantial Order service and clearance sufficient for Council business.
KnowledgeMastery of the Compact, Restricted Histories, Council procedure, and the classified material necessary for the House's mandate.
CertificationThe Order may certify eligibility and security standing but cannot reject a lawful House choice merely because other Houses dislike the appointee.
House autonomyThe Compact regulates eligibility; each sovereign Great House decides its own appointment process.

General Cessation Directives

When a major surface war or comparable conflict has ceased to serve tolerable Order interests, the Council may issue a General Cessation Directive with at least seven affirmative votes out of nine. The directive does not require the Houses to adopt a common public position, abandon all private interests, or disclose their influence. It does require every House to cease materially prolonging the conflict for unilateral advantage and to use its available leverage toward an end consistent with the Order’s collective decision.

Failure to comply is treated as an Order offense because it places a surface allegiance or private House advantage above a lawful Council determination concerning the common survival and stability of the hidden system. The Conclave retains extraordinary review authority: eight of eleven Great-House Voices may rescind, narrow, or materially amend a directive, but review does not suspend compliance in the meantime.

03

The Grandmaster

The Grandmaster presides over the international Order and serves the Order as a whole rather than exercising a House mandate. The Grandmaster may not simultaneously serve as a voting Councilor or exercise a Great House's Conclave Voice, and may not simultaneously hold the office of Head of a Great House.

House identity and relationships do not disappear when a Grandmaster assumes office; the constitutional safeguard is separation of authority, recusal where required, and the inability to cast the Grandmaster's office as an extra House vote.

04

Offices Are Not Yet Assigned

The 2026 holders of most Conclave delegate, Councilor, and Deputy Councilor roles remain intentionally unassigned while current family lines and dossiers are still being developed. A missing officeholder on the archive is not a vacancy in-world unless explicitly stated.
