Institutional Framework

A common system built by sovereign Houses to preserve themselves.

The Order exists because the Great Houses learned that secrecy, shared rules, adjudication, limited restraint, and collective action are necessary to prevent rivalry from destroying the dynasties themselves. It is not a moral authority above the Houses. Each House remains the primary object of its members’ loyalty; the Order is the framework through which the Houses manage coexistence, common threats, and mutually necessary limits.

The Compact

The constitutional body of agreements, revisions, judgments, and protocols governing the Veil, House sovereignty, the Common Peace, shared obligation, and the limits of authority.

Conclave & Council

Every Great House has one Voice in the Conclave. Nine Great Houses hold standing Council mandates and govern the common international affairs of the Order.

Induction

Induction is a reciprocal relationship, not merely disclosure of secrets. The inductee receives trust, protection, access, education, and opportunities appropriate to the relationship; in return, the inductee accepts binding obligations of secrecy, conduct, and Compact responsibility. A sponsoring patron vouches for the candidate, and the sponsoring House ultimately accepts responsibility for the relationship.

Clearance

Security clearance and compartment access are not the same thing. High access in one field does not imply broad authority elsewhere.

Authority

Lethal, paramilitary, intelligence, patronage, educational, financial, and social-development authority are separately tracked where relevant.

House Autonomy

The Order does not ordinarily choose a House Head, dictate family governance, or control lawful internal succession. It intervenes when conduct crosses into Compact jurisdiction.

House Primacy

Members are loyal to their House first. The Order exists because every Great House has a long-term interest in preserving a system that limits mutually destructive conflict and protects the hidden world on which all Houses depend.

The Surface World

Governments, corporations, militaries, parties, churches, and other public institutions are external to the hidden constitutional system. A House may work through them, but it does not treat them as superior to House loyalty.

01

Representation

In the Conclave, the House Voice belongs by right to the Head of each Great House. The Head may exercise it personally or accredit a Conclave Delegate and Alternate Delegate. The vote remains the House's, not the delegate's.

A Council mandate belongs to the elected Great House. The House appoints a qualified Councilor and Deputy Councilor according to its own internal law. The Order certifies eligibility and security standing but does not choose the House's representative.

No person may exercise more than one Great House's constitutional Voice simultaneously. The Grandmaster cannot simultaneously act as a voting Councilor, exercise a House Voice, or serve as Head of a Great House.

02

House Loyalty & Compact Obligation

Primary loyalty belongs to the House. The Order does not ask a Wycliffe, Falkenrath, Ravenscroft, Volkov, or other Great-House member to regard an international institution as more sacred than the dynasty itself. The Compact is instead a binding agreement among sovereign Houses that each accepts because the system protects long-term survival, secrecy, recognition, sanctuary, and limits on ruinous competition.

A House may still choose to breach the Compact if it judges an existential House interest more important. Doing so does not make the breach lawful; it means the House has knowingly accepted the consequences imposed by its peers. The system functions because the costs of abandoning it are normally greater than the gains from defection.

Surface loyalties remain subordinate to the House. A member may sincerely serve a country, military, ministry, corporation, profession, church, or political cause, but none outranks the dynasty. The modern shorthand is: “House before all; the Compact is how the Houses survive one another.”

03

Induction as Reciprocal Trust

Formal induction tells a person: you are being brought inside because this relationship is valuable to both sides. For a blood member, induction formalizes responsibilities that House formation has been preparing them to assume. For a spouse, patron, protégé, specialist, ally, or other outsider, induction is a deliberate extension of trust and a recognition that a deeper relationship benefits both the individual and the sponsoring House.

Benefits vary by status and depth of relationship rather than arriving as a single package. They may include protected access to House and Order networks, patronage, professional opportunity, education, security assistance, sanctuary eligibility, institutional advocacy, medical or legal support, introductions, privileged information, and the ability to participate in parts of the hidden system unavailable to outsiders.

Those benefits create obligations. Every inductee accepts responsibility for the Veil, the portions of the Compact applicable to them, protected information, sanctuary rules, conflicts of interest, and the consequences of misusing House or Order access. Deeper induction means broader access and stronger institutional protection, but also greater accountability and a wider field of obligations.

Induction does not by itself create House blood, House membership, clearance, office, or operational authority. Those remain separate statuses. A deeply inducted outsider may be highly trusted without becoming a constitutional member of the sponsoring House.

04

Patronage & Internal Jurisdiction

A patron who brings an outsider into the Order acts as sponsor and surety. Independent vetting protects the House from obvious mistakes, but sponsorship still means that a named person and ultimately a named House stand behind the inductee.

The sponsoring House has first jurisdiction over misconduct by its members and sponsored inductees. Every Great House therefore maintains an internal mechanism—council, tribunal, chamber, review board, or equivalent—to investigate breaches, restrict access, impose sanctions, protect the Veil, and decide whether a relationship can be repaired or must be severed.

The Order normally expects the House to solve its own problem before international institutions become involved. International jurisdiction begins when the matter crosses House boundaries, affects common Order interests, threatens the wider Veil, or demonstrates that the House is unwilling or unable to discharge its responsibility.

05

The Histories

Common Histories are taught to every inducted member and establish the shared historical framework behind the Compact: major House conflicts, extinct and fallen Houses, elevations and degradations, famous alliances, succession crises, Veil failures, constitutional revisions, and the development of the Long Peace.

Restricted Histories reveal deeper political and operational context as members advance. Sealed Histories remain compartmented and may contradict or materially complicate the common account.
