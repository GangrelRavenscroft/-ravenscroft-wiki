Institutional Framework

An international structure distinct from any one House—and superior to every surface state.

The Order exists to maintain the common framework between powerful dynasties: secrecy, constitutional law, inter-House peace, shared security, protected institutions, adjudication, and international obligations. Its members do not treat governments, parties, corporations, militaries, churches, or ordinary civic institutions as constitutional superiors. Within the hidden system, the Compact governs the Houses; lawful House authority governs House members; surface institutions are instruments, environments, and temporary structures to be influenced, used, obeyed, evaded, or opposed as circumstance requires.

The Compact

The constitutional body of agreements, revisions, judgments, and protocols governing the Veil, House sovereignty, the Common Peace, shared obligation, and the limits of authority.

Conclave & Council

Every Great House has one Voice in the Conclave. Nine Great Houses hold standing Council mandates and govern the common international affairs of the Order.

Induction

Every inducted member learns the Compact and the Histories. Ravenscroft blood members normally encounter initial induction around eighteen and deeper adult formation around twenty-one.

Clearance

Security clearance and compartment access are not the same thing. High access in one field does not imply broad authority elsewhere.

Authority

Lethal, paramilitary, intelligence, patronage, educational, financial, and social-development authority are separately tracked where relevant.

House Autonomy

The Order does not ordinarily choose a House Head, dictate family governance, or control lawful internal succession. It intervenes when conduct crosses into Compact jurisdiction.

Hierarchy of Allegiance

Compact obligations outrank House commands; lawful House obligations outrank ordinary public, professional, ideological, and state loyalties. External office never excuses betrayal of the House or violation of the Compact.

The Surface World

The Order preserves influence through secrecy rather than overt sovereignty. States and public institutions are managed as part of the surface world; allowing one of them to penetrate, capture, or subordinate a House is itself a grave constitutional failure.

01

Representation

In the Conclave, the House Voice belongs by right to the Head of each Great House. The Head may exercise it personally or accredit a Conclave Delegate and Alternate Delegate. The vote remains the House's, not the delegate's.

A Council mandate belongs to the elected Great House. The House appoints a qualified Councilor and Deputy Councilor according to its own internal law. The Order certifies eligibility and security standing but does not choose the House's representative.

No person may exercise more than one Great House's constitutional Voice simultaneously. The Grandmaster cannot simultaneously act as a voting Councilor, exercise a House Voice, or serve as Head of a Great House.

02

Hierarchy of Allegiance

The Order teaches a simple constitutional hierarchy: the Compact stands above every House; each House stands above the surface institutions to which its members may temporarily belong. A member may sincerely serve a country, military, ministry, university, corporation, profession, church, political movement, or other public institution, but those loyalties remain subordinate when they conflict with lawful House duty or Order law.

This doctrine does not require constant defiance of public law. Secrecy, prudence, legitimacy, personal conviction, and strategic usefulness often make ordinary compliance preferable. The distinction is constitutional rather than theatrical: the Order does not concede that a surface government possesses ultimate authority over a Great House merely because that government claims jurisdiction.

Conversely, House loyalty is not absolute against the Order. A Head cannot authorize a Compact violation simply by calling it House policy. The modern formulation is: “No temporal allegiance shall supersede the House, and no House allegiance shall supersede the Compact.”

03

The Histories

Common Histories are taught to every inducted member and establish the shared historical framework behind the Compact: major House conflicts, extinct and fallen Houses, elevations and degradations, famous alliances, succession crises, Veil failures, constitutional revisions, and the development of the Long Peace.

Restricted Histories reveal deeper political and operational context as members advance. Sealed Histories remain compartmented and may contradict or materially complicate the common account.
