# GREAT HOUSE PAGE STANDARD

## DEFINITIVE DEPTH POLICY — USER-LOCKED, 11 OCTOBER 2026

**House Ravenscroft is the ONE dynasty for exhaustive worldbuilding.** This explicitly supersedes any older passage below that treats every Great House as requiring Ravenscroft-level dossiers, exhaustive family trees, extensive historic chronicles, financial ledgers, institutions, charters, multi-batch character portraits or lengthy branch narratives.

### Tier A — House Ravenscroft (deep treatment)
Develop as fully as useful to the narrative: detailed generations, characters and personal Master A/B dossiers, genealogies, formative history, Order offices and clearance, patronage, private intelligence, finances and holdings, residences, political relationships, visual identities and scenes. Preserve all existing locked content and the separate dossier standard.

### Tier B — All other Great Houses (concise but credible treatment)
Each of the other ten recognized Great Houses merits a **cohesive, attractive overview**, not encyclopedic development:
- basic identity, geography, distinctive competence and historic character;
- Head and immediate senior leadership / a small number of noteworthy relatives **only when already established or narratively needed**;
- principal seat and one or two important institutions and sources of power;
- broad history and contemporary geopolitical role, major relationships and relevant pressures;
- approximate House-scale wealth **only when established and meaningful**;
- a **compact subordinate-House directory** with names, region/industry and superior;
- **standard illustrated Great-House package**: elaborate and simplified matching heraldry, Head and immediate ruling family (including individual images where useful), ancestral/constitutional seat, institutional headquarters, influence map and a defining House-level scene. Preserve already approved faces and sites; do not substitute only a crest/seat for the fuller House-level set.
  
For non-Ravenscroft Great Houses, **the full 21-section required page order and stress-test documentation below are a reference menu, NOT a mandatory exhaustive production checklist**. Condense, combine or omit nonessential sections. We may build enough credibility for all eleven to coexist in the Order, but our creative time is concentrated on Ravenscroft. Do not keep expanding other House genealogies simply to fill perceived gaps.

### Tier B visual and ruling-household package — USER CLARIFICATION, 11 OCTOBER 2026

**IMPORTANT: “Concise” does NOT mean minimal visuals or an anonymous Great House.** User explicitly confirmed that the **other ten Great Houses retain the same core family, location and illustrated House-package depth already established for the first Houses**, while only Ravenscroft receives exhaustive multi-generation and individually deep dossier treatment.

For each non-Ravenscroft **Great House**, normally retain or create the following **House-level visual/canon essentials** when going through its visual development:
1. **Full formal heraldic achievement** — elaborate House crest and supporters where visually appropriate; distinct, historical-feeling, readable, matching family identity.
2. **Simplified heraldic seal / crest** — clearly derived from the SAME approved shield and colors; suitable for mastheads and person dossier headers, not an unrelated monogram.
3. **Current Great-House Head and immediate ruling family** — names, appropriate personal details, ages, apparent ages, roles, and a manageable selection of photorealistic cinematic portraits/family image(s). Approved faces and longevity canon remain immutable; new faces are proposals until approved.
4. **Principal ancestral / constitutional House seat** — architecture, grounds and one defining establishing exterior; historically credible for country and family.
5. **Primary institutional / administrative seat** — distinct city-facing residence or headquarters with an establishing exterior.
6. **House influence map** — core/deep influence/overlap/legacy network distinctions without representing national governments as privately owned.
7. **At least one defining historical or institutional image** when helpful to the Great House's identity, without extensive subordinate family image batches.

This is comparable to the established Great-House set for Wycliffe, Falkenrath, Veyrholm and Valdieri, **not** the exhaustive Ravenscroft family-dossier/character-photo scope. A **modest organized batch set** covering these Great-House subjects is welcome; do **not** forbid batches as such. Do not require detailed dossiers or portraits for subordinate Major/Lesser Houses just because a few were previously archived.

**Visual continuity:** keep full and simplified heraldry visually consistent; differentiate ancestral seat vs administrative headquarters; preserve already approved Henri Alexandre de Villiers portrait and any other locked face; age other Great-House blood members consistent with approved longevity benefit (Ravenscroft strongest, others slightly less). Separate image identity approval and asset upload/path mapping from concept approval. Never claim temporary concept image is locked official heraldry before the user selects it.

### Tier B cross-House layout and information parity — implemented 12 October 2026

For all **developed non-Ravenscroft Great Houses**, share an understandable layout and field pattern while preserving each House's approved imagery, distinctive culture, terminology, and additional story-specific chapters.

- **Canonical topical destinations:** identity/qualification, history, geography/influence, institutions/resources, ranked cadet/Major/Lesser network where applicable, Order, inter-House relationships, House governance, leadership and immediate family, strengths/vulnerabilities, visual canon, and locked/open canon status. A House may retain additional subject-specific sections and nested galleries. Anchor IDs and nav labels must resolve; include a consistent footer link back to the House Directory and page top.

- **Visual canon discipline:** each approved unique PNG should remain reachable from its House record with working full-resolution links and alt text. ### Tier C — Major and Lesser Houses under ANY Great House (directory only)
Standard output: **House name · rank/immediate superior where needed · principal locality/House-network sphere · industry or specialty**. One table row or sentence is sufficient. **No individual family dossiers, Head identities, spouses, descendants, personal finances, estates, corporate ownership registers, charter history, portraits, heraldry, or dedicated webpages by default.**

**Grandfather clause:** already locked named subordinate Heads, children, records, financial decisions or images stay canon and accessible in archival files; never erase approved continuity. But do **not** feature their detailed biographies in routine Great-House overviews, generate additional subordinate pages, or continue subordinate projects without an explicit user request. This applies to previously expanded Valdieri and Belgian/Swiss de Villiers subordinate families as well.

**No extra workstreams:** Do not treat unapproved individual subordinate-character, succession, charter, financial or portrait proposals as necessary unfinished work. When moving through the roster, stop at appropriate world-level coverage and return creative focus to Ravenscroft.

## Purpose
Great House pages are institutional records, not person dossiers. They explain why a House qualifies as a constitutional peer, how it developed historically, where and how it exercises influence, how it relates to the Order, and how its identity differs from other Great Houses.

## Production scope — LOCKED user instruction, 11 October 2026

**Great Houses receive full development; subordinate Major and Lesser Houses receive SHORT DIRECTORY ENTRIES ONLY.** This instruction applies to **ALL recognized Great Houses** and supersedes older production requirements in this file insofar as they demand full biographies, detailed cadet charters, exhaustive subordinate enterprise/finance registers, separate portrait sets, or stand-alone websites for Major and Lesser Houses.

**Minimum entry for each Major or Lesser House:**
- **House name**;
- **rank/status and immediate superior** (briefly, if not obvious);
- **locality / primary area of House-network responsibility**;
- **principal industry, specialty or local mandate**.

A table row or a one-sentence entry is sufficient. For example: **House de Bréval — bound Major under de Villiers; Belgium (Wallonia/Brussels); industrial infrastructure and cross-border rail/logistics**. **House de Cressens — Lesser under de Corvelles; Geneva; engineering-contract liaison and compliance**.

**Do not automatically create** named Heads, spouses, children, personal histories, individual fortunes, property inventories, dedicated pages, portraits, house crests, technical dossiers, bespoke governing councils or additional subordinate Layers. No need to assign a net worth or personal Head to every subordinate. If a Head, branch history, asset or family detail is *already approved*, **preserve it in archival canon** and do not delete or retcon it; simply keep routine Great-House public-facing sections concise. Expand a particular subordinate only on explicit user direction or when indispensable to a specific story.

**Priorities:** Great-House history, sovereign ruling family, actual main institutions, long-range power and politics, estates, history, wealth and visuals. Subordinate Houses provide *geographic and functional coverage*, not a second tier of full worldbuilding projects.

**Constitutional constraints still apply:** all bound or cadet **Major Houses report directly to the Great House**; Lessers report to their specified Major or directly to the Great House. House territory describes responsibility within the hidden network, not sovereignty over modern states, public institutions or whole industries. **Universal criminal rule:** organized crime in recognized House territory must submit to its Great House and pay tribute upward through the hierarchy. Refusal is punished and persistent defiance crushed; submission preserves ordinary semi-autonomy. Territory is not the same as an atlas influence map. See `world/house-criminal-vassalage-and-tribute.md`. Avoid inventing local officers and charters merely to complete a template.

## Great-House stress-test categories (high-level summary for non-Ravenscroft peers)
Every recognized Great House must have a credible historical answer for:
1. Independent sovereignty
2. Durable succession
3. Institutional depth
4. Strategic consequence
5. Independent resources
6. Protection and security
7. Network and patronage
8. Order reliability / ability to carry common obligations

This is not a point system. Extraordinary wealth, age, military strength, or prestige cannot compensate for the absence of genuine independent sovereignty or durable succession.

## Required wealth accounting and cadet separation

The House's **principal net worth** means beneficial wealth of the ruling/senior line and genuinely common sovereign House patrimony **net of liabilities**, excluding property separately owned by any cadet House. Cadet branches are distinct subordinate Houses with their **own net worth figures**, even where they contribute enormous strategic power to the parent House. Bound Major/Lesser Houses, patronage families, unaffiliated outside shareholders, third-party client assets, charitable assets held for other beneficiaries and independent allies' property are likewise not automatically owned by the principal House.

House records must keep these distinct:
1. Senior-family personal/household net worth, with jointly owned property reconciled;
2. Separately held *common principal-House* trusts, assets and reserves actually attributable to it;
3. Each cadet House's independently attributable net worth, **not included in (1) or (2)**;
4. Portfolio enterprise value, gross revenue, managed assets, client assets and ownership stakes (only beneficial equity belongs in net worth);
5. Broader resources under control or substantial influence, **overlapping and not additive** to wealth;
6. Political, industrial, covert and technological leverage that cannot be meaningfully described as personal dollar wealth.

**World scale:** In the 2026 setting most recognized Great Houses possess fortunes comparable to or above the richest families of the contemporary real world, along with much greater strategic and institutional power. Ravenscroft is the overwhelming strategic leader, with existing multitrillion-scale wealth descriptions and specific relatives who individually possess hundreds of billions. Valdieri is an intentional, constitutionally equal but unusually weak restored Great House. Do not create equal wealth for all eleven by formula or inflate existing individual corporations to fit headline figures.

For approved 2026 financial ranges, use the <a href="../world/great-house-wealth-revaluation-proposal.md">Great-House Wealth Canon</a>. The principal-House bands for Wycliffe, Veyrholm, Falkenrath, Volkov and Valdieri, and the independent Genova and Canavese bands, are **locked**. Earlier four-House $8–22B estimates are superseded legacy figures. **Ravenscroft has no locked principal-only net worth or wider assets-under-influence range** pending review of its trusts, personal fortunes, institutions and cadet property. Preserve named companies, individual fortune facts and exact ownership canon until independently reconciled.

## Great-House power and dependencies (summary only outside Ravenscroft)

Every Great-House record should describe its broad network of subordinate Houses, institutions, independent allies, patronage and commercial/covert relationships **at the Great-House level**. Use **brief table rows only** for individual subordinate Major/Lesser Houses. Do not produce a proportional full power register for each subordinate House.

**Cadet House rank rule (locked world canon):** A genuine cadet House is a **distinct subordinate House with either Major or Lesser standing**. Cadet status describes blood descent and additional family privileges; it does **not** add a rank between the Great and Major Houses. All subordinate **Major Houses answer directly to their Great House**, including Major-ranking blood cadets and unrelated bound Major dynasties. Major Houses may in turn govern Lesser Houses, but **not other Major Houses**. **Major Houses administer regional House interests; Lesser Houses administer local territories or specialist industries/professions.** A specialist Lesser's business may cross regional boundaries without changing constitutional allegiance. No House's assigned area means that it owns the public government or all regional enterprises. A Lesser cadet follows the same chartered allegiance rules as other Lesser Houses. Every House maintains separate ownership, Headship, personnel and succession; collateral family households are not automatically cadets.

### Explicit relationship categories

- **Constitutionally subordinate:** Major-ranked cadet and non-blood bound Major Houses (each directly to the Great House), and Lesser-ranked cadet or bound Houses (direct to Great or through their chartered Major superior); identify Head, rank, direct superior, reserved charter powers and appeal routes.
- **Formally allied or protected:** an autonomous dynasty or institution joined by a treaty, protection agreement or reciprocal service; no implied sovereign command.
- **Owned or governed:** House-controlled corporations, estates, trusts, funds, hospitals, universities and foundations; establish actual voting, appointment or fiduciary power.
- **Invested or commercially linked:** equity stakes, joint ventures, capital support, contracts, industry relationships, supply chains; distinguish ownership from supplier or partner status.
- **Patroned or sponsored:** people, families, companies, organizations and careers benefiting from reciprocal patronage; patronage need not include induction.
- **Politically influential:** policy coalitions, officials, public institutions, civic bodies and professional organizations; distinguish access and alignment from actual command.
- **Covertly linked or criminal:** distinguish local Great-House territorial authority over organized crime (compulsory), the actual Major/Lesser or syndicate superior, criminal ownership, obligations, tribute, semi-autonomy, defiance, inter-House exceptions and whether participants know about the Order.
- **Independent or contested:** rivals, unaligned institutions and overlapping spheres where more than one House has meaningful relationships.

### Required regional and local/specialist map

For every subordinate **Major House**, identify **only the House name, status, geographic sphere and principal industry/function**, plus immediate allegiance where useful. Every Major reports **directly to the Great House**, regardless of cadet or nonblood ancestry. Do not develop each Major's separate leadership, capital, security, branch charters and family tree as a default output.

For each **Lesser House**, specify its **name, locality or industry/specialty and immediate superior** (Great House or Major House). One short row is enough. Specialists may serve projects across regional boundaries without changing allegiance.

House jurisdiction is different from state sovereignty, ownership of a city/industry, patronage, board representation or informal influence. Mark cross-regional institutional cooperation and escalation of charter disputes separately.

For each major real or fictional institution record **type of relationship, relative power, office or House that owns the tie, actual mechanism of authority or influence, independence/limits, obligations, vulnerabilities and canon certainty**. Different Houses must make different choices about which levers they use and how much they rely upon them. An institution can have multiple ties to different Houses. Never treat an entire country, government, region, industry or criminal underworld as automatically **owned** by one family. Authority over organized crime in recognized House territory nevertheless remains compulsory; influence alone does not establish territorial jurisdiction.

The shared reference is <a href="../world/great-house-power-structure.html">Great-House Power Structure</a>. The House-specific section should give a tangible, distinctive network, not merely repeat the world guide or list ungrounded assets.

## Cadet-branch relations (brief general note outside Ravenscroft)

Great-House pages may include **one concise paragraph** describing the sovereign family's general relationship to cadet branches or subordinate Houses if relevant. **Do not create separate cadet-relationship subsections or full histories for subordinate Major/Lesser House entries**; the only standard presentation for each subordinate is its name, locality, specialty and parent relationship.

Distinguish **formal House constitution** from **unwritten political management**:

1. **Cadet identity and holdings.** Treat recognized cadet branches as genuine subordinate Houses (not merely collateral family groupings), with their own leadership, succession, holdings and dependent networks, comparable in operation to a Major House but constitutionally subordinate. Distinguish independent or bound Major Houses, separate Great Houses, ordinary collateral households and patroned families.
2. **Succession and charter authority.** Explain who authenticates the House Head, appoints regents, recognizes cadet officers, adjudicates disputes, and controls whole-House protected instruments under the <a href="../order/dynastic-continuity.html">Articles of Dynastic Continuity</a> (1953–1956). A former cadet's genuine independent sovereignty can still potentially qualify for separate Great-House recognition.
3. **Unwritten loyalty methods.** Describe the particular House's mix of elite education and kinship formation; patronage and career access; branch capital and shared financial guarantees; social precedence, marriage alliances and symbolic surety; security clearance and information compartmentalization; professional cross-appointments; continuity and scrutiny of branch leadership.
4. **Actual power distribution.** Cadets must retain credible positive reasons to remain, enough delegated authority to operate, and understandable reasons why the senior line retains ultimate constitutional leadership. Do not automatically turn every cadet into a powerless dependent or every Head into an omniscient intelligence master.
5. **Distinctive temperament.** Detail how the House applies rewards, personal loyalty, status, family culture, enforceable charters, social influence, discipline and rare coercive exceptions. Distinguish what is publicly acknowledged inside the Order from what only senior members privately understand.
6. **History and current tensions.** Include at least one concrete example of a past cadet crisis, a negotiated accommodation, a restoration of trust, or a standing source of grievance where appropriate. Identify whether a proposed practice is locked, inferred or open.
7. **Boundaries of knowledge and authority.** Blood descent, marriage, patronage, wealth, office, Order rank, security clearance and actual covert access are separate dimensions. Junior or married-in Ravenscrofts must not automatically know historic classified operations.

**World guide:** <a href="../world/cadet-houses-and-loyalty.html">Cadet Houses and Quiet Loyalty</a> is the shared worldbuilding reference for formal-vs-shadow governance. Include or cross-reference it where needed.

**World-wide canon:** Following the Valdieri civil war, the ratified Articles of Dynastic Continuity provided shared succession/cadet-governance standards. Great Houses also use varied **unwritten** systems of loyalty and strategic dependence. These are common patterns, not an unofficial universal Compact. Different Houses may pursue very different approaches. Political fosterage or child wardship is not an automatic or universal House custom; Valdieri's historic Genova settlement is an exceptional coercive instance.

**Ravenscroft-specific reference:** See the <a href="../houses/ravenscroft-cadet-relations.html">Ravenscroft Cadet Relations record</a> when created or updated; preserve its existing patronage and Ashbourne doctrine rather than inventing contradictory education or patronage rules. Separate the main-line/collateral-family relationships from patroned outsiders and independent former offshoots.

Where no named cadets exist, a short note such as **Cadet Houses not yet established** is sufficient. Do not invent a genealogy or additional Houses to fill a table.

2. Principal seat — exterior or establishing image.
3. House influence map — must distinguish core, deep influence, overlap/contested areas, and legacy networks; never imply modern sovereignty unless canon explicitly does.
4. Defining historical image — one scene/event that explains the House’s modern identity.

### Optional / recommended
5. Modern institutional image — financial district, university, foundation, port, research center, etc., depending on House identity.
6. Current House Head portrait — only after the character has approved visual canon.
7. Current Councilor / delegate portraits — only after those characters exist.
8. Major secondary seat or protected Order site.
9. Historically important marriage/alliance image if central to House history.

Avoid generic image galleries. Every visual should explain Great-House identity, geography, history, leadership or institutional power. **Subordinate Major and Lesser Houses do not require portraits, individual estate views or heraldry.** Preserve previously approved art if it exists without making more.

## Influence-map rules
- “Core” = deepest historical/institutional concentration, not political ownership.
- “Deep influence” = unusually dense House networks and durable relationships.
- “Overlap/contested” = more than one Great/Major House has serious capacity.
- “Legacy network” = historic relationships remain but modern dominance has faded.
- Local Major/Lesser Houses and independent lineages continue to exist inside Great-House spheres.
- Do not color entire countries as possessions unless the legend makes the distinction explicit.

## House-history standard
Great-House history should explain:
- origin and independence;
- elevation/recognition where known;
- defining crises;
- major wars, assassinations, alliances, marriages, and Compact precedents;
- periods of ascendancy or decline;
- adaptation to public historical change;
- present Long Peace posture;
- unresolved grievances that still shape 2026 politics.

Do not make every Great House ancient, equally wealthy, equally global, or equally competent. Different routes to peer status are desirable.

## Style
Dark navy/black, antique gold, cream serif, restrained heraldic/intelligence-archive aesthetic. House pages should feel broader and more constitutional than person dossiers.

Use full heraldic crest on House records. Person dossiers retain the simplified crest where appropriate.

## Canon control
End each House record with Locked / Open blocks. “Open” identifies details intentionally left for later development, not contradictions or missing canon.
