# GREAT HOUSE PAGE STANDARD

## Purpose
Great House pages are institutional records, not person dossiers. They explain why a House qualifies as a constitutional peer, how it developed historically, where and how it exercises influence, how it relates to the Order, and how its identity differs from other Great Houses.

## Required page order
1. House masthead and full heraldic identity
2. Global archive navigation
3. Breadcrumb
4. House section-jump navigation
5. House hero / identity statement
6. At-a-glance status rail
7. House Identity / philosophy
8. Great-House Qualification stress test
9. Historical Development timeline
10. Geography & Influence
11. Institutional / economic / strategic power base
12. Subordinate-House and External Power Structure (REQUIRED)
13. Order role and Long Peace posture
14. Inter-House Relations / alliances / rivalries
15. Governance & Representation
16. Cadet Branches: Formal Authority and Unwritten Loyalty (REQUIRED)
17. Family / senior lines when available
18. Strengths & Vulnerabilities
19. Visual Canon
20. Canon Control
21. Bottom jump navigation + Back to Top

## Required constitutional fields
- Recognition status
- Core geographic sphere
- Principal seat
- Institutional/operational center if distinct
- Great-House qualification summary
- House Head when established
- Conclave Voice holder (normally Head)
- Standing Conclave Delegate and Alternate when established
- Council mandate status
- Councilor and Deputy Councilor when established
- Order relationship
- Major Compact precedents involving the House, if any

Do not invent officeholders merely to fill the page. Use “intentionally unassigned / record pending” until the relevant character exists.

## Required Great-House stress-test categories
Every recognized Great House must have a credible historical answer for:
1. Independent sovereignty
2. Durable succession
3. Institutional depth
4. Strategic consequence
5. Independent resources
6. Protection and security
7. Network and patronage
8. Order reliability / ability to carry common obligations

This is not a point system. Extraordinary wealth, age, military strength, or prestige cannot compensate for the absence of genuine independent sovereignty or durable succession.

## Required House-wide power and dependencies register

Every Great-House record must describe more than family governance. Show the House's relationships with **cadet Houses, subordinate/bound Major Houses, Lesser Houses, independent allies, patroned families and individuals, corporations and trusts, industrial interests, political networks, civic institutions, and criminal or covert organizations when established**. Major and Lesser Houses receive a proportional version.

**Cadet House clarification (locked world canon):** A true cadet House is a **distinct subordinate House**, with its own Head, succession, property, institutions, internal staff, affiliated families, patronage and dependent networks, operating much like a Major House **under the Great House's overarching constitutional sovereignty**. It has the additional blood-family benefits and responsibilities conferred by its lineage and charter. A collateral relative or family household is **not automatically a chartered cadet House**. A subordinate Major House is a separate dynasty and is not transformed into a blood cadet by taking an oath or receiving patronage.

### Explicit relationship categories

- **Constitutionally subordinate:** chartered cadet House, bound Major House, bound Lesser House; identify Head, charter, reserved powers and appellate or oversight obligations.
- **Formally allied or protected:** an autonomous dynasty or institution joined by a treaty, protection agreement or reciprocal service; no implied sovereign command.
- **Owned or governed:** House-controlled corporations, estates, trusts, funds, hospitals, universities and foundations; establish actual voting, appointment or fiduciary power.
- **Invested or commercially linked:** equity stakes, joint ventures, capital support, contracts, industry relationships, supply chains; distinguish ownership from supplier or partner status.
- **Patroned or sponsored:** people, families, companies, organizations and careers benefiting from reciprocal patronage; patronage need not include induction.
- **Politically influential:** policy coalitions, officials, public institutions, civic bodies and professional organizations; distinguish access and alignment from actual command.
- **Covertly linked or criminal:** independent clandestine contacts, criminal-business families, or organizations with a supported fictional relationship; distinguish alliance, transactional cooperation, rivalry and direct affiliation.
- **Independent or contested:** rivals, unaligned institutions and overlapping spheres where more than one House has meaningful relationships.

For each major real or fictional institution record **type of relationship, relative power, office or House that owns the tie, actual mechanism of authority or influence, independence/limits, obligations, vulnerabilities and canon certainty**. Different Houses must make different choices about which levers they use and how much they rely upon them. An institution can have multiple ties to different Houses. Never treat an entire country, government, region, industry or criminal underworld as automatically owned by one family.

The shared reference is <a href="../world/great-house-power-structure.html">Great-House Power Structure</a>. The House-specific section should give a tangible, distinctive network, not merely repeat the world guide or list ungrounded assets.

## Required cadet-branch relations and private loyalty architecture

Every newly developed Great House **must** include a meaningful section on how its senior line relates to cadet branches, even if the family currently has no large established cadet branch. This is a standard requirement for every future House we develop and for substantial revisions of older House pages. **Apply a proportionate version to Major and Lesser House records as well**, identifying parent-House obligations, subordinate family divisions and loyalty practices where relevant.

Distinguish **formal House constitution** from **unwritten political management**:

1. **Cadet identity and holdings.** Treat recognized cadet branches as genuine subordinate Houses (not merely collateral family groupings), with their own leadership, succession, holdings and dependent networks, comparable in operation to a Major House but constitutionally subordinate. Distinguish independent or bound Major Houses, separate Great Houses, ordinary collateral households and patroned families.
2. **Succession and charter authority.** Explain who authenticates the House Head, appoints regents, recognizes cadet officers, adjudicates disputes, and controls whole-House protected instruments under the <a href="../order/dynastic-continuity.html">Articles of Dynastic Continuity</a> (1953–1956). A former cadet's genuine independent sovereignty can still potentially qualify for separate Great-House recognition.
3. **Unwritten loyalty methods.** Describe the particular House's mix of elite education and kinship formation; patronage and career access; branch capital and shared financial guarantees; social precedence, marriage alliances and symbolic surety; security clearance and information compartmentalization; professional cross-appointments; continuity and scrutiny of branch leadership.
4. **Actual power distribution.** Cadets must retain credible positive reasons to remain, enough delegated authority to operate, and understandable reasons why the senior line retains ultimate constitutional leadership. Do not automatically turn every cadet into a powerless dependent or every Head into an omniscient intelligence master.
5. **Distinctive temperament.** Detail how the House applies rewards, personal loyalty, status, family culture, enforceable charters, social influence, discipline and rare coercive exceptions. Distinguish what is publicly acknowledged inside the Order from what only senior members privately understand.
6. **History and current tensions.** Include at least one concrete example of a past cadet crisis, a negotiated accommodation, a restoration of trust, or a standing source of grievance where appropriate. Identify whether a proposed practice is locked, inferred or open.
7. **Boundaries of knowledge and authority.** Blood descent, marriage, patronage, wealth, office, Order rank, security clearance and actual covert access are separate dimensions. Junior or married-in Ravenscrofts must not automatically know historic classified operations.

**World guide:** <a href="../world/cadet-houses-and-loyalty.html">Cadet Houses and Quiet Loyalty</a> is the shared worldbuilding reference for formal-vs-shadow governance. Include or cross-reference it where needed.

**World-wide canon:** Following the Valdieri civil war, the ratified Articles of Dynastic Continuity provided shared succession/cadet-governance standards. Great Houses also use varied **unwritten** systems of loyalty and strategic dependence. These are common patterns, not an unofficial universal Compact. Different Houses may pursue very different approaches. Political fosterage or child wardship is not an automatic or universal House custom; Valdieri's historic Genova settlement is an exceptional coercive instance.

**Ravenscroft-specific reference:** See the <a href="../houses/ravenscroft-cadet-relations.html">Ravenscroft Cadet Relations record</a> when created or updated; preserve its existing patronage and Ashbourne doctrine rather than inventing contradictory education or patronage rules. Separate the main-line/collateral-family relationships from patroned outsiders and independent former offshoots.

For a House with few or no named cadets, provide an explicit **Cadet Relations — Developing** section that describes known policy, historical precedent and unanswered structural questions instead of fabricating a complete genealogy. When cadets are named later, update this section.

## Visual standard
### Required
1. Full heraldic crest — primary House identifier; full crest reserved for House-level use.
2. Principal seat — exterior or establishing image.
3. House influence map — must distinguish core, deep influence, overlap/contested areas, and legacy networks; never imply modern sovereignty unless canon explicitly does.
4. Defining historical image — one scene/event that explains the House’s modern identity.

### Optional / recommended
5. Modern institutional image — financial district, university, foundation, port, research center, etc., depending on House identity.
6. Current House Head portrait — only after the character has approved visual canon.
7. Current Councilor / delegate portraits — only after those characters exist.
8. Major secondary seat or protected Order site.
9. Historically important marriage/alliance image if central to House history.

Avoid generic image galleries. Every visual should explain either identity, geography, history, leadership, or institutional power.

## Influence-map rules
- “Core” = deepest historical/institutional concentration, not political ownership.
- “Deep influence” = unusually dense House networks and durable relationships.
- “Overlap/contested” = more than one Great/Major House has serious capacity.
- “Legacy network” = historic relationships remain but modern dominance has faded.
- Local Major/Lesser Houses and independent lineages continue to exist inside Great-House spheres.
- Do not color entire countries as possessions unless the legend makes the distinction explicit.

## House-history standard
Great-House history should explain:
- origin and independence;
- elevation/recognition where known;
- defining crises;
- major wars, assassinations, alliances, marriages, and Compact precedents;
- periods of ascendancy or decline;
- adaptation to public historical change;
- present Long Peace posture;
- unresolved grievances that still shape 2026 politics.

Do not make every Great House ancient, equally wealthy, equally global, or equally competent. Different routes to peer status are desirable.

## Style
Dark navy/black, antique gold, cream serif, restrained heraldic/intelligence-archive aesthetic. House pages should feel broader and more constitutional than person dossiers.

Use full heraldic crest on House records. Person dossiers retain the simplified crest where appropriate.

## Canon control
End each House record with Locked / Open blocks. “Open” identifies details intentionally left for later development, not contradictions or missing canon.
