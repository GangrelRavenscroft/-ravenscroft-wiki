Power That Moves Through Institutions

The Houses govern dynasties, but influence whole societies.

A Great House is a sovereign dynastic center surrounded by subordinate Houses, independent allies, owned institutions, patronage relationships, commercial partners, political networks and clandestine contacts. These ties overlap. Constitutional sovereignty, ownership and influence are not the same kind of control.

01

Constitutional Houses and Subordinate Households

The sovereign Great House is the highest House-level constitutional authority within its own dynastic sphere. It may include distinct subordinate cadet Houses, formally bound Major Houses, and subordinate or client Lesser Houses. These are not synonymous. Cadet status signifies blood kinship and an inherited charter, not a separate constitutional rank. A cadet House can have Major or Lesser standing. All subordinate Major Houses, blood cadets and non-blood bound Houses alike, answer directly to their Great House; a Major House may have Lesser dependents but does not rule another Major House. Independent Major Houses remain constitutionally outside that subordinate hierarchy. Some Major and Lesser Houses remain autonomous, are allied rather than subordinate, or have different relationships with multiple Great Houses.

The parent House's Head does not run every subsidiary household. True cadet Houses normally have their own Head, household, succession, internal council or officers, private assets, protections, employees, patronage relationships and subordinates, much like a Major House. Their charter reserves parent-House supremacy in specified matters: central dynastic allegiance, final whole-House succession recognition, common obligations, and defined overarching security or adjudication jurisdiction. A cadet may be prosperous and consequential without holding a Conclave Voice or International Council mandate of its own.

Family blood grants a cadet potential advantages unavailable to a bound outsider House: participation in dynastic ceremonies, shared formation, particular succession rights where the charter allows, access to extended-family institutions, and stronger personal protections. None of these automatically provides sensitive clearance or authority over the parent Great House.

02

Five Different Kinds of Control

Constitutional subordination

Recognized House charters, allegiance, delegated sovereignty, obligations and adjudication. Appropriate for cadet and certain bound Houses; not a synonym for ownership.

Legal or economic control

Equity, trusts, property ownership, governance appointments, voting rights, financing agreements and contractual duties. Corporate control depends on specific actual rights.

Patronage and protection

Sponsorship, opportunities, education, capital, protection and reciprocal service. Can exist without Order induction and does not necessarily convey command.

Political and cultural influence

Reputation, personal loyalty, common interests, institutional access and coalition-building. Strong influence remains less certain than enforceable authority.

Covert power

Discreet intelligence access, clandestine alliances, protected staff and occasionally coercion. Expensive, deniable in the narrative, politically risky and never assumed to be universal or infallible.

The same target may have several ties to one Great House at once, or ties to multiple Houses. The archive must specify which tie is real instead of merely writing that a dynasty “controls” it.

02A

Major Houses as Regional Authorities; Lesser Houses as Local or Specialist Authorities

Major-House standing conveys regional administrative responsibility within the Great House's dynastic network. Whether a blood cadet or unrelated bound dynasty, a subordinate Major answers directly to the sovereign Great House. Its Head coordinates regional House security, Lesser-family charters, civic and political relationships, industrial obligations, protected institutions and patronage within a meaningful geographical sphere. Majors retain their own fortunes, internal succession and professional institutions.

Lesser-House standing conveys local territorial authority or a specialist industry/professional mandate. Territorial Lessers may serve a town, cluster of municipalities, county, port, valley or defined House district; specialist Lessers may focus on engineering, metrology, finance, conservation, logistics or another industry, serving projects across several Major regions without acquiring constitutional authority over those regions. Some Lessers report to their assigned Major House; others directly to the Great House.

These are jurisdictions within the House and Order, not ownership of civil governments. Public officials and private businesses remain separate actors. Major regional authority does not make every business, property or politician in a region subordinate. Business relationships may overlap: constitutional allegiance stays with each House's chartered superior, and a Great House resolves disputed territorial or professional mandates between its peer-level Majors.

02B

Layered Territorial Administration — The Feudal and Federal Analogy

Common world-wide House doctrine

The Great House is the apex of a delegated territorial and institutional hierarchy. A useful analogy is a hidden feudal polity (king, duke, count, baron) or a federal-to-local administration (federal, state, county). These are analogies of scope and delegation, not literal public crowns, government offices, or an assertion that every House has the same number of subordinates. There is no new required rank between Great, Major and Lesser.

Great House — central and retained domains

The sovereign Great House holds ultimate dynastic supremacy over its bound network and can directly administer its own principal estates, city districts, projects, protected institutions, or professional mandates. It has multiple Major Houses and may also charter directly subordinate Lesser Houses and enterprises without routing them through a Major.

Major House — regional and delegated domains

Each bound Major House answers directly to its Great House; it ordinarily holds meaningful regional authority, manages an area or institutional mandate in its own name, and may have multiple subordinate Lesser Houses. A Major also directly administers parts of its remit instead of assigning every locality to a Lesser House. Major Houses do not become vassals of other Majors.

Lesser House — local and specialist domains

Each bound Lesser House is chartered either directly beneath the Great House or beneath an assigned Major House, with its own Head and locally meaningful autonomy. A territorial Lesser may administer a locality, valley, district or protected institution; a specialist Lesser may work across regions without acquiring another Major's constitutional territory. Not every district must have its own Lesser House.

Administration is not a complete land partition. A Great House, a Major House and a Lesser House may each personally administer some holdings while delegating other parts of their responsibilities. A geographic sphere can be discontinuous; enclaves, negotiated boundaries, specialist overlays and external alliances are possible. Specific charters determine who has the primary supervisory authority, reserved rights, appeals and succession recognition. Those powers cannot be inferred only from physical proximity or the breadth of a colored influence map.

Political boundaries and assets remain distinct. House-administered 'territory' means the internal jurisdiction of a hidden dynastic network—its protected holdings, designated obligations, influence relationships and authorized operations—not civil sovereignty, public tax authority, jurisdiction over ordinary residents, or ownership of the public government. A Major's land and independently owned businesses do not become the Great-House Head's personal assets merely because the Major is subordinate. Likewise, a senior House can set reserved common obligations without personally managing every lesser household.

03

Cadet Houses: Real Subordinate Dynasties

A chartered cadet House is a distinct subordinate House at Major or Lesser standing, not simply a group of cousins. Major-standing cadets answer directly to their Great House; Lesser-standing cadets answer directly to the Great House or through their chartered Major superior. The cadet designation records kinship and special family duties, not an additional tier of rank. Its Head governs the branch's internal affairs, appointments, lesser clients, daily business, residence, security and subordinate networks within the terms of its charter. In practice it can resemble a capable Major House with additional blood membership and prestigious access.

The Great House retains a defined constitutional relationship through recognition of the cadet Head and lawful succession, specified reserved acts, common House obligations, appeals or mediation, and eligibility for shared educational and financial institutions. The senior line cannot simply treat private cadet property as its own by preference alone. Charters may be revised by established procedures, and genuine separation remains possible in principle, as illustrated by Ravenscroft's historic origin as a former Falkenrath cadet.

Even a well-established cadet may possess bound Lesser Houses, attached businesses, investment partnerships, and its own layers of patrons or underworld relationships. Those ties do not become direct personal assets of the Great-House Head. The House can require compliance with overarching obligations without managing every relationship itself.

04

Bound Major Houses and Lesser Houses

A subordinate Major House (including a Major-ranking blood cadet) answers directly to a sovereign Great House and may have Lesser Houses beneath it, but cannot be constitutionally beneath another Major House. Non-blood bound Majors maintain their distinct dynastic origins. It may retain its Head, succession, property, institutions, subordinate families and local sphere, yet owe defined service, strategic consultation, political alignment, tribute or reciprocal protection. The precise terms matter. A powerful bound Major House may possess more operational resources than a small cadet but fewer blood-family privileges.

Independent or allied Major Houses must remain separate from this category. A Great House may support or cooperate with them through treaties, patronage, finance or common projects without acquiring constitutional jurisdiction over them. Lesser Houses may answer to a cadet House, a Major House, or directly to a Great House; they can specialize in local governance, professional services, protected estates, small commercial domains or regional patronage. Greater proximity does not always mean greater importance.

Such bonds can be hereditary or time-limited, strong or weak, affectionate or exploitative. Inter-House status, legal rights and actual obedience are related but distinct and must not be reduced to one arbitrary ranking.

05

Corporations, Trusts, Banks and House Institutions

Institutions require a separate ownership map from the constitutional genealogy. Some companies are fully House-owned, some majority-controlled, some held through family or institutional trusts, some financed or partnered, and many merely do business with House interests. Legitimate executives, boards, shareholders, employees, regulators and commercial counterparties continue to matter. Public corporate form is not a magical extension of House commands.

Great Houses may maintain portfolio offices, professional management teams, investment bodies, technical institutes, philanthropic foundations, research laboratories, historical estates and private protection capabilities. A cadet or bound Major House can separately own or administer similar entities. A House's control of an institution depends on the actual lawful or clandestine relationship: property rights, governance appointment, contracts, patronage or direct command. One organization may serve several Houses without belonging to any one of them.

06

Industrial Power Is a Network, Not a Monopoly

To dominate a sector, a House need not own every participant. Its influence may arise from a portfolio of manufacturing expertise, capital reserves, trained personnel, patents, university relationships, logistical infrastructure, credible professional management and durable commercial partnerships. Competitors, governments, technological change and other Houses place real limits on that influence.

Every House should have a recognizable industrial or institutional lane, but domains overlap. Veyrholm's logistical and communications leverage does not imply universal ownership of ports; Valdieri's long-term finance and specialist production do not imply all Italian industry obeys it; Ravenscroft's breadth does not mean that every sector is equally under its influence.

07

Governments and Political Networks

House influence in the public political world is usually mediated through individuals and institutions with their own incentives: elected officials, administrators, advisers, civic leaders, researchers, donors, law firms, foundations, industry organizations and foreign-policy relationships. Influence may be strong or durable but is not equivalent to owning a government. Officials can disagree, change sides, lose office or respond to competing pressures.

A Great House may have preferred policy outcomes, trusted contacts, long-standing personal relationships and institutions operating within a political sphere. Some political actors may know of the Order; many do not. The archive should explicitly separate alignment, access, influence, patronage, and actual direction, which is rare and requires a canon basis.

07A

Public-Sector Institutional Power

Government networks are an important component of Great-House power at national, regional and municipal levels, including executive, legislative, judicial, regulatory and public-safety institutions. Ravenscroft represents the exceptional case of enough interlocking institutional relationships to exercise decisive practical influence over American policy and enforcement without commanding every official or formally constituting the government. Other Houses may possess considerable influence but should not automatically be credited with the same reach. Ravenscroft governmental canon.

08

Criminal Organizations and Covert Power

Criminal power is not one standardized subsidiary of every Great House. Some Houses maintain limited clandestine capabilities within their own security or intelligence apparatus; others have relationships with independent criminal-business families, underworld syndicates, illicit brokers, or specific individuals. Such groups may cooperate for money, protection, access, shared interests or fear, but an alliance is not the same thing as operational ownership.

Some organizations are genuinely independent and may resist House pressure or work for several powerful patrons. Overreliance on criminal networks invites exposure, divided loyalties, violence and breaches of the Compact. The Veyr family network, for example, is an established criminal-business dynasty with significant legitimate commercial interests and relationships; its existence must not be silently converted into direct House ownership or a generic subordinate company. Covert and criminal activities should be treated as political and moral dimensions of the fictional setting, not assumed to be controllable without cost.

08A

Parallel Underworld Hierarchies and Compartmented Patronage

Distinct from House constitutional ranks

A criminal enterprise may attach at any House level. A large syndicate or criminal-business network might report, contract or owe obligations directly to a Great House; another enterprise may be attached to a Major House; a smaller local group may be subordinate to a Lesser House. No scale mechanically determines allegiance, and the nature of each relationship must be specified: command, owned interests, finance, protection, contractual access, patronage, alliance, or indirect influence. Underworld groups do not automatically become constitutional Houses.

Criminal organizations may also govern criminal subordinates of their own. For example, a larger organized-crime network might exercise authority over a regional group, which might in turn direct or patron a local street gang. Their internal tiers do not map one-for-one to Great/Major/Lesser rankings. Authority can be delegated down several levels in the underworld while its ultimate House affiliation sits elsewhere. Particular structures vary from tightly controlled to loosely affiliated or independent.

Knowledge is deliberately uneven. Members of a street gang may know only their direct leader or neighboring syndicate contact, not the regional patrons behind them, the House involved, the Order, or the ultimate limits of the chain. Even a powerful criminal organization may know its immediate House contact but not that contact's superiors. Awareness, oath, induction, membership, criminal collaboration, wealth and operational clearance remain different categories. Some actors know more, and discovery, betrayal and divided loyalties remain plausible story consequences.

There is no single universal crime pyramid. Rival organizations, entirely independent groups, changing agreements, competing patrons, and informal or cross-House links exist. A House's indirect patronage should not be mislabeled as exclusive ownership or infallible command. The same territorial hierarchy can support legitimate businesses, professional networks, intelligence contacts and criminal relationships, each with its own reporting and knowledge structure.

Record separate chains in the lore: (1) House constitutional superior, (2) any territorial mandate, (3) legal/beneficial ownership, (4) operational or criminal reporting superior, (5) patronage or protection ties, and (6) what each organizational tier actually knows. Unknown or unapproved links must remain unknown rather than filled in by assumption.

09

Security, Intelligence and the Limits of Knowledge

House security offices, cadet protective teams, professional intelligence staff and external contacts may operate in overlapping jurisdictions. The central Great House normally guards whole-House succession, the most sensitive archives, inter-House obligations and strategic security authority; cadet or bound Houses retain real but limited protective and operational capacity under their own charters and agreements.

Clearance follows task, office, need and trust, not merely blood or wealth. A powerful cadet Head may be fully informed about its own networks and unaware of a senior-line clandestine operation. An outsider may work closely with a House without ever learning the Order exists. Elias Alexander Ravenscroft's lack of knowledge of the historic covert Valdieri campaign illustrates this limitation.

10

How to Record a House's Power Without Inventing Control

A standardized Power and Dependencies Register should accompany substantial House pages. Each real organization or subordinate family receives a relationship designation: cadet, bound Major, bound Lesser, allied, patroned, owned, majority-controlled, minority-invested, contracted, politically connected, covertly affiliated or rival. Multiple designations can coexist, but should be explained.

Record the controlling or responsible office, actual legal rights where known, public footprint, House-related resources, degree of institutional independence, who benefits, explicit limits and unresolved loyalties. Distinguish the parent Head from cadet Heads and professional managers. A claim of covert influence requires a canon basis and should not be inferred from geography, common surname or industry alone.

11

Different Great-House Methods

Ravenscroft: a wide system of capital, elite formation, alliances, opportunity, patronage and strong compartmented central security; distinct subordinate cadet Houses remain attractive places to build power. Falkenrath: strong branch charters, dynastic hierarchy, territorial and industrial institutions. Wycliffe: fiduciary relationships, legal authority and prestigious institutions. Veyrholm: shared routes, infrastructure, communications and logistical coordination. Valdieri: reconstruction through formal cadet restraints, patient capital and technical independence after a ruinous civil war.

These are common directions and previously established tendencies, not a full operating manual for each House. Other Great Houses, including Volkov, Ashcroft, Shen, Kuroda and Amaru, may differ substantially; individual methods and relative severity require their own canonical House passes.

11A

Institutional Influence, Compartmentalization and Geographic Depth

Great Houses can influence the same independent corporation through financing, supply dependencies, board and executive relationships, technical research, public-sector relationships, patronage and covert sources at once. Independently reporting people inside one institution may be unaware of each other, and can provide separate observations of the institution and of one another. An inducted family member is already formed and loyalty-tested, but remains subject to scrutiny; vital protégés, uninducted allies and consequential outsiders may merit especially intensive intelligence attention. A House usually has the deepest and most redundant institutional network close to its influence cores, particularly major cities, with exceptional distant strategic nodes. None of these relationships automatically establishes public sovereignty or constitutional command. Read the locked institutional influence and intelligence doctrine.

11B

Distributed Reach Through Major and Lesser Houses

Great-House influence includes affiliated Major and Lesser Houses, not simply central House personnel and assets. Subordinates extend regional, specialist and institutional networks while retaining their own legal property and constitutional obligations. A long-established Great House can therefore have at least some low-intensity institutional reach throughout its home country even where no senior-House offices stand. This does not mean control of every municipality, business or public body. National reach is uneven and subject to exceptions: postwar Valdieri is not assumed equally influential throughout Italy; Volkov's western Russian cadet and professional networks do not support a blanket map of all Russia. See the locked institutional influence doctrine.

12

Canon and Open Design Decisions

Locked framework: Cadet Houses are distinct subordinate Houses with their own leadership and material network, closer to a Major House in practical operation than a mere collateral household, with additional dynastic rights and obligations. Bound and independent Major Houses are different; Lesser Houses can be nested under several lawful authorities. Great-House networks include multiple kinds of legal, economic, social, political and covert influence; control is not interchangeable with mere proximity. The Articles of Dynastic Continuity remain common Order law, while shadow loyalty practices vary.

Further development: A specific Great House's ranking of methods, named subordinate dynasties, claimed territories, exact office powers, percentages of ownership, political networks, criminal affiliations, procurement or industrial dependencies, and formal rights of exit must be individually established. No unnamed House, company, official or syndicate is made a confirmed subordinate solely by this general guide.
