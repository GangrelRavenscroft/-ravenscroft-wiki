Setting Frame

A contemporary world with an older hidden architecture.

Public life remains recognizably modern. Behind it, Great Houses, Major and Lesser Houses, allied dynasties, and the international Order maintain long-lived systems of family governance, patronage, influence, intelligence, education, security, and common law.

Visibility

The Houses are not theatrical secret societies. Their companies, universities, foundations, estates, careers, and public institutions are real. Hidden power works through ordinary legitimacy rather than replacing it.

Great Houses

Great-House standing is formal constitutional recognition of an established peer. Wealth or antiquity alone are insufficient. Read the recognition standard.

The Order

The Order is not owned by any one House. It provides the common framework governing secrecy, inter-House peace, shared institutions, international security, adjudication, and obligations that no House can safely administer alone.

The Compact

The central principle is simple: the Houses govern themselves; the Order governs the peace between them. Open the Compact overview.

Governance

Every Great House possesses one Voice in the Conclave. Only nine Great Houses at a time hold International Council mandates. See Conclave and Council rules.

Technology

House capabilities are somewhat ahead of the public baseline in surveillance, drones, AI-assisted analysis, secure systems, and longevity-related medicine while remaining contemporary rather than overt science fiction.

01

The Long Peace

The modern era is an unprecedented period of cooperation among the Great Houses. Direct Great-House war has not vanished from memory, but sustained open conflict between peers has become extraordinarily rare. The Compact and postwar Order institutions have pushed most rivalry below the threshold of general House war.

Assassination, intelligence competition, economic pressure, patronage contests, political maneuvering, and proxy conflict still occur. Much contemporary violence is concentrated among Lesser Houses, sometimes with larger powers quietly supporting opposing sides.

The Long Peace is therefore not an absence of conflict. It is the successful containment of conflict below the threshold of Great-House war. In 2026 the system remains functional, but tension is rising and no one can assume the present balance is permanent.

02

Order Formation

Initial induction

Ravenscroft blood members normally receive initial induction around age eighteen, though pathways differ across Houses and non-House members. Every inductee receives formal instruction in the Compact and the Common Histories.

Deeper formation

Deeper adult formation commonly begins around twenty-one for Ravenscroft members. Restricted Histories, specialist knowledge, warrants, clearance, and compartment access develop separately and are never assumed from blood alone.

Shared baseline: anyone who truly joins the Order learns at least the Compact and the Histories. Rank, wealth, House identity, clearance, patronage, and operational authority remain separate categories.
03

2026 Great-House Framework

The current working peer roster contains eleven recognized Great Houses: Ravenscroft, Wycliffe, de Villiers, Falkenrath, Volkov, Veyrholm, Valdieri, Amaru, Ashcroft, Shen, and Kuroda. Nine of the eleven hold International Council mandates at any one time.

The roster is constitutionally settled for current worldbuilding use, while several Houses still require full historical profiles demonstrating how and when they satisfied the Great-House standard. Council assignments will be reconciled after that historical stress test.

04

Ravenscroft in the Modern Order

House Ravenscroft began as an American cadet branch of House Falkenrath and broke from Falkenrath authority during the American War of Independence. It spent the following decades establishing durable independence before receiving Great-House recognition in the early nineteenth century.

Ravenscroft did not become the most influential Great House merely by becoming Great. Its modern primacy emerged during the post-World War II rise of the United States, with Alexander Ravenscroft instrumental in converting American industrial, financial, scientific, strategic, educational, and institutional expansion into durable House influence. Primacy is political and practical, not constitutional, and can be lost.
