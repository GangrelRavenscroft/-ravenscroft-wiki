# Québec and the Open Great-House Peerage — World Canon, 2026

**User correction ratified 17 October 2026.** Supersedes earlier eleven-House limit and Ravenscroft–Wycliffe contested-Québec entries.

## Québec belongs to Great House de Villiers

The supplied North America Great-House influence atlas identifies Québec with a **de Varenne** sphere. The name **de Varenne** was subsequently eliminated as an *out-of-universe authoring choice* and replaced everywhere by **Great House de Villiers**. Accordingly, **de Villiers holds Québec's primary hidden territorial criminal jurisdiction**, not Ravenscroft or Wycliffe. No de Varenne dynasty survived or transferred territory *within* the fictional history.

Ravenscroft controls the United States and most western/central Canada through Ontario; Wycliffe controls Atlantic Canada. Each can retain substantive influence, relationships, investments and professional contacts in Québec, but cannot claim the superior territorial authority assigned to de Villiers. Specific Quebec local jurisdictions, subordinate House assignments and negotiated exceptions are still open, not new inventions made from an atlas's color.

## No cap on recognized Great Houses

**The number of Great Houses is open-ended.** Earlier archive documents had named eleven as a working list; this does not establish a constitutional ceiling. The developed/named examples include Ravenscroft, Wycliffe, de Villiers, Falkenrath, Volkov, Veyrholm, Valdieri, Amaru, Ashcroft, Shen and Kuroda. They are not an exhaustive limit.

The supplied North America atlas also depicts **House Xolotl** as an important Mexican-core Great House. Preserve this identity and regional premise while its full recognition chronology, family line and exact jurisdiction are reconciled in subsequent work; do not erase it merely to fit a supposed eleven-House roster.

The **Conclave** comprises all recognized sovereign Great Houses with one Voice apiece. The **International Council** comprises **nine elected Great-House mandates** and can have fewer mandates than there are Great Houses. Neither the Nine nor an old working roster restricts how many recognized Great Houses may exist.
